If Everything Else Were Forgotten, This Is What Would Be Worth Keeping — and One More Thing, From Further Down the Road, That No List Could Reach
I. The Irreducible Core
  1. 01You're not a machine wearing out. You're a fire that needs feeding, or it goes out.
  2. 02There's no neutral setting. Every system is growing or shrinking, right now.
  3. 03Reserve is built in months and lost in weeks. Guard your streak: it's the actual asset.
  4. 04Recovery isn't time off from the work. It's the second half of it.
  5. 05Feeling fine isn't evidence the buffer is full, only that it isn't empty yet.
  6. 06Push hard where the window is wide. Go often but light where it's narrow.
  7. 07Pain arrives late in tendon and bone. By the time it speaks, the damage is already old.
  8. 08A chain fails at its slowest link, not its most tired one.
  9. 09Track the capacity, not the mood. A number doesn't lie to you the way a feeling can.
  10. 10You're not aging on autopilot. You're negotiating, every day, whether you notice or not.
Ten lines, if that's all you need this morning.
But there's one more thing worth keeping, from further down the road than a list can reach.
II. A Letter Forward

Dear Ricky,

I don't know what your Tuesday looks like right now, but I remember what it looked like once. Early alarm. The ruck or the weights or the walk, whichever the week called for. A supplement pile that took its own kind of discipline just to keep straight. A hundred lectures, if you can believe that, read one at a time, mostly because you actually wanted to know, not because anyone made you.

I want to tell you it was worth it, and I want to tell you why, and it's not the reason you might expect.

It wasn't the numbers. It was never really the numbers. It was every ordinary morning since then that you got to have. The ones that felt like nothing at the time — coffee, a decent night's sleep, the energy to actually want to do something with the day instead of just getting through it. Thousands of mornings like that, stacked up so quietly you'd never have called any single one of them a victory. But here I am, and they add up to something. They added up to this.

You gave yourself so much good, ordinary time. Time to get better at things. Time to become more patient than you started out. Time to be around for the parts of life that only show up if you stick around long enough to see them. Nobody hands you that. You built it, deliberately, one unglamorous choice at a time, on days when it would have been so easy to skip it.

And I want you to know it worked in a way no biomarker ever measured. You got to feel good in your own body for a long stretch of years. You got to move without thinking about it, sleep without dreading the morning, show up for people with something left in the tank instead of running on fumes. That's the actual prize. Not a chart. A life that felt like yours, for a long, long time.

So here's what I actually want to say, from all the way over here: thank you. Thank you for taking it seriously back then, when it would have been so much easier not to. Thank you for the early mornings and the boring consistency and the patience with a process that never once promised you a finish line. You didn't do it for me, not really — you did it for whoever you'd get to be. But I'm the one who gets to enjoy it, and I don't take that lightly.

It was a good bet. It paid off exactly the way you hoped — in mornings, in energy, in years that felt full instead of just long.

Keep going a little further. It's been worth every bit of it.

With real gratitude,Ricky, at 70
